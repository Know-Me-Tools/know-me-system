The company of one.

How a single operator runs strategy, finance, marketing, product, design, engineering, security and customer intelligence as governed agent teams — with KnowMe as the personal cockpit and the Universal Agent Runtime (UAR) and its fabric doing the work under authority you issue, scope and revoke.

Proposal · no implementation changes Source: UAR governed agent teams research Confidence 0.86 · verification partial 60 indexed sources
01 · THE PREMISE

One accountable human. Many offices. Every office is a governed team.

A solo business still has the functions of a company: someone sets strategy, someone watches cash, someone ships product, someone talks to customers, someone worries about security. In a company of one, you hold every office. The goal is not to invent a crowd of executive personas. It is to give each office a reusable, governed agent team that does the work, while you keep the decisions that carry real authority.

The research behind this brief makes the order of operations explicit: governance closure → lossless definitions → bounded teams → durable business workflows → consented executive representation → federation. A roster of executive "twins" before those foundations is the wrong way round. That is even more true for a solo operator: when one person holds every office, a badly scoped CEO agent effectively holds all of them.

The solo-operator rule

Create a team only when parallel work, independent review, or separate authority justifies the coordination cost. Most days, one agent with the right skill is enough. Multi-agent mode has to earn its place in accepted-result quality or time, against a single-agent baseline.

02 · KNOWME AS COCKPIT

KnowMe is where the person lives. UAR is where the work runs.

In the proposed split, KnowMe supplies consented context and personal representation. It is the surface where you see what your teams are doing, answer approvals, set goals and decide what each agent may know about you. It does not run the teams. That boundary is what lets the business outlive any single device, and it is what keeps your private memory out of your business agents.

Context

Consented personal context

Your memory, notes and preferences stay yours. Business agents get an explicit, scoped slice under a representation grant, never your whole history because you hold an office.

Attention

One approvals inbox

One approval request per external action, visible in KnowMe and The Boss alike. No duplicate queues. Routine steps run under standing scoped grants so you are not asked twice.

Direction

Goals and engagements

Objectives and engagements are gated state machines (prometheus-container). Agents propose and cite evidence. Completing a commitment stays a human act.

Rhythm

Morning brief

Your operating review: what each team finished, what is waiting on you, which effects are in an unknown state, and which grants are about to expire.

Identity

Agents are principals

Each deployed agent instance gets its own did:key. Every governed action carries you as subject and the agent as actor, so "what did this agent do on my behalf" is a query.

Placement

Same contracts on every device

An embedded UAR kernel on mobile, and full UAR on desktop or server. Capabilities are negotiated. A high-assurance profile never silently downgrades on a phone.

04 · WHAT YOU ACTUALLY OWN

Definitions travel. Identity and authority stay home.

Replicating an organization is mostly a modeling problem. The research separates things that are usually collapsed into one "agent" string. For a solo operator, this separation is what lets you share a team template with a peer, hire your first employee into an office, or sell the business without leaking your personal history or your authority.

Why this matters specifically when you are solo

  • Your first hire is a role assignment, not a migration. The office's role memory, team and workflows move with the OrganizationalRoleAssignment. Your personal KnowMe history and representation profile do not. The successor inherits the office, not your persona.
  • Sharing a team template is safe by construction. Send a peer your marketing TeamDefinition and their install gets new identities and their own grants.
  • Team membership never unions capabilities. Putting the Finance analyst and the Brand designer on one launch team does not give the designer finance credentials or the analyst publish rights.
  • A team can be independent peer roots. Do not force them into one parent tree just to reuse child messaging. Authorize a team mailbox explicitly. A lead can still spawn children to satisfy one team task.

Evaluate your twin like any other claim

Person-specific simulation has empirical support on bounded behavioral prediction, not on executive judgment under novel pressure. Test your advisor on held-out decisions: factual recall, disagreement rate, false claims of authority, abstention quality, sensitive disclosure, style fidelity and correction retention. Disagreement is useful signal, so do not tune for flattering agreement. You can correct or retire the profile at any time.

07 · STAYING IN CONTROL

Every boundary must allow. Any deny wins. Recheck right before the effect.

Authenticate the deployed agent instance, not a role name the model supplied. The trusted host builds principal, action, resource and context itself. A definition's declared capabilities set an upper bound on the request. They are then evaluated against every live boundary.

Govern the control plane, not just tool calls

Registering definitions, activating instances, changing team membership, assigning tasks, spawning children, peer messaging, memory access, subscriptions, skill installation, policy changes and issuing or revoking grants are all explicit actions with resource scopes. An agent allowed to execute a task cannot, by that permission, edit its own definition, join a team or issue a grant.

The solo separation-of-duties problem

The research sets a hard rule: when an action needs two people, both approvers must be distinct authorized humans, and neither the requester nor its twin can fill a slot. In a company of one, no internal pairing can satisfy that rule. Plan for it rather than paper over it.

This brief's proposal For money movement and other high-impact effects, name an external second party (your bookkeeper, accountant, or a bank's own dual-control feature) as the second approver. Keep standing grants under explicit ceilings. Consider a mandatory cooling-off window with re-evaluation for anything above the ceiling. These are design options to test, not findings of the source research.

Cedar is the decision engine, not the ledger

  • Atomically reserve spend and quotas, resolve role assignments and revocation, validate data classification and record approvals outside the evaluator, then pass the authoritative results into policy.
  • In governed profiles, reject invalid policy deployments and deny on relevant evaluation errors. A Cedar error on a forbid policy is not automatically a whole-request deny, so the host has to choose that posture explicitly.
  • A read grant plus a write grant can add up to a leak. Carry trusted data labels and provenance, and check egress by destination and payload. Cedar evaluates the labels it is given. It does not find sensitive content on its own.
  • Treat connector outputs, customer messages and skill instructions as untrusted input.
08 · A DAY IN THE COMPANY

From one customer message to a shipped improvement, with one decision for you.

The recommended first slice is the product-feedback loop. It crosses every team boundary, produces real external effects and never touches money. Here is how it runs in a company of one.

Observers without accidental authority

  1. Subscribe to a logical instance, team or workflow, optionally filtered by conversation, event type, labels and lifecycle state. The subscription is a host-authorized record: principal, tenant, filters, delivery mode, cursor, expiry, replay range and granted actions.
  2. Authorize four things independently: creating the subscription, delivering an event, expanding the payload and taking the resulting action. Observing a channel does not grant permission to post to it.
  3. Carry lineage on every event: event ID, origin instance, conversation/workflow/run IDs, causation and correlation IDs. Use inbox deduplication, bounded replay, backpressure, dead letters and hop limits.
  4. Replays inform. They do not re-act. Old events can feed analysis but must never automatically repeat an external action.
  5. Revocation stops future delivery and actions and invalidates cached sensitive access where it can. It does not pretend that already disclosed information can be recalled.
09 · HOW AGENTS STAY ALIVE

"Stateful" is not "a process that stays up."

Invocation style, context retention, persistence, placement and authority are independent axes. A stateless worker can serve a durable state machine, and a resident process can lose everything on a crash. For a solo operator this matters in practice: your laptop sleeps and your phone goes offline, but your workflows should not care.

11 · BUILD SEQUENCE

Governance first. Your twin comes near the end.

PhaseExit evidence
0 · InventoryEvery entry point mapped to an action gate; the implemented/specified/proposed distinction approved
1 · GovernanceNegative tests across managed, direct, embedded and remote routes; invalid policy and revoked grants cannot cause protected effects
2 · DefinitionsRound-trip fixtures authoring → IR → persisted artifact → effective runtime capability; unsupported mandatory semantics block installation
3 · TeamsCrash and reassignment, concurrency, tenant isolation, parent cancellation, peer-mailbox tests
4 · WorkflowsDuplicate, reordered and replayed event tests; uncertain-effect recovery
5 · RepresentationYou review held-out scenarios; zero simulated approvals accepted as human approvals
6 · FederationThe same contract suite passes on local, embedded and cloud; unsupported profiles are visibly refused

Use isolated worktrees per repository with a common initiative name and a manifest pinning each repo's branch and commit. Matching branch names do not make a cross-repository change atomic. Coordinate dependency pins instead of forcing one project's versions on another.

Measure against a single-agent baseline with the same models, tools and budgets: accepted-result quality, your correction time, unauthorized effects blocked, recovery correctness, duplicated or unknown effects, token cost, latency, interoperability loss and administration burden. A coding score does not prove finance readiness.

13 · OPEN DECISIONS

What still has to be decided.

From the research

  • Which deployment profiles must support Cedar and durable team state, especially embedded/mobile?
  • Which current callers depend on the direct tool path, and how should their migration be staged?
  • Which SaaS adapters provide reliable idempotency or reconciliation?
  • Who is entitled to issue executive grants, approve personal representation, and retain the resulting artifacts?
  • What trust evidence is required before protected work goes to an external runtime?
  • Which throughput and recovery requirements justify adopting a separate durable workflow engine?

Specific to the company of one This brief

  • Who is your external second approver for money movement, and how does KnowMe represent that person's approval?
  • What are the ceilings on standing grants for routine effects, and do they show in the morning brief?
  • Which offices get a Level 2 advisor profile, if any, and which stay pure role assistants?
  • Where does the line between personal KnowMe memory and business role memory sit on the day you hire someone?
Decision

Proceed with the architecture direction. Close the source-confirmed governance and conversion gaps before expanding authority. Keep one UAR execution host, keep teams separate from thread trees, and make placement replaceable through contracts. Ship the product-feedback team first.
